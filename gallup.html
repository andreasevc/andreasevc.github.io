What is PCA, and what am I looking at?

Each person in this analysis is described by 34 numbers (their talents ranked 1 - most significant to 34 - least significant). That's a 34-dimensional profile, which is impossible to plot directly. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a technique that squashes high-dimensional data down to 2D so we can actually see it.

The trick: instead of just picking two talents and ignoring the other 32, PCA finds the two directions through the full 34-dimensional space where people differ from each other the most, and projects everyone onto those two directions. Think of it like photographing a 3D sculpture - you can only capture 2D in a photo, so you'd choose the angle that reveals the most interesting structure. PCA picks that "best angle" mathematically, but in 34 dimensions instead of 3.

What this means for the chart below:

  • The axes don't represent any single talent - they're abstract combinations of all 34, weighted to maximize how spread out people appear.
  • Close dots = people with overall similar talent profiles, even if their top-5 lists look different on paper.
  • Distant dots = genuinely different ways of thinking and working.
  • Clusters hint at groups of people who'd likely approach problems in similar ways.

Computed in-browser: each row is centered, the n×n Gram matrix is formed, and the top two eigenvectors are extracted by power iteration with deflation. The 2D coordinates are the corresponding singular-value-scaled scores. No external linear-algebra library.

Talents that only one or two people in the department carry in their top 5. If those people are unavailable for an assignment, that strength essentially leaves the department - useful to know when scoping a project that needs them, or when thinking about cross-training and resilience.

Distinctiveness

For each person, the sum of 1 ÷ (how many people share each top-5 talent), across their five signature strengths. Higher = a profile that is harder to replicate from the rest of the department. A score near 5 would mean every one of their top-5 talents is unique to them; a score near 1 means each is widely shared. Chips show the talent and the freq within the 23-person department.
